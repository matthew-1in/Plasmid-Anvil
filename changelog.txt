PLASMID&ANVIL CHANGELOG
Newest first. One entry per version number; changes that ship without a number
bump fold into the entry above them.


v5.2
Construction files read as well as write, and three fixes to what v5.1 shipped.

- A construction file can be pasted in and becomes the pipeline it describes: one card per step,
  wired together, with the sequences it declares dropped into the slots, and capsule templates
  saved under Saved capsules. Anything that cannot be read is reported with the line it is on
  and what is wrong with it, rather than a blank refusal. The reader takes tabs or spaces, the
  SOE and PCA lines this app writes as comments, and untyped declarations, so a file exported
  here comes back as the pipeline it left as.
- Slots that were added can be taken off again. The x on an empty fragment, enzyme or necessary
  input removes that slot, down to the number a fresh card of that kind comes with; on a filled
  slot it still only clears what is in it.
- The information button on a capsule card opened nothing. A stray name in the dialog's title
  threw before the dialog was built, so the capsule flowchart never appeared; the same fault
  stopped Show capsule flowchart in the inspector and Show flowchart on a saved capsule.
- Export view wraps a long name onto the next line and grows the box to fit, instead of cutting
  it short, and a box can be clicked to select its step exactly as a bubble can in standard
  view. The drawing itself is unchanged, and a downloaded SVG still carries no selection.


v5.1
Capsules: a whole pipeline packaged as one card, and flowcharts you can put in a report.

- A pipeline can be turned into a capsule. Every slot the pipeline had filled itself becomes an
  input on the front of one card, the wiring between the steps is kept inside, and running it
  hands back the products you chose when you made it. A capsule behaves like any other
  experiment card: it can be stacked into a pipeline, and it can go inside another capsule.
- Capsules are saved under Saved capsules on the Cloning tab, with folders of their own. A card
  carries its own copy of what is inside it, so deleting the stored capsule does not break work
  already on the bench, and reordering a card's inner steps changes that card alone.
- A capsule is written in a construction file as a template: a header line naming the capsule
  and its inputs, the steps it is made of (referring to those inputs, and to intermediateN for
  anything made and used on the way), and a Product line naming what comes back. A pipeline that
  uses a capsule writes every template it needs first, innermost capsule first, then calls the
  capsule like any other step, ending in the product name or a comma-separated list of them.
  Both line shapes are this app's extension to the format: a file that calls a capsule runs here
  and not in the C6 reference, which knows the operations rather than capsules.
- Capsule cards carry the same information button as a pipeline, with the flowchart of the steps
  inside and the same order and input controls.
- Flowcharts have a second view. Export view redraws the same graph as an engineering flowchart:
  rectangular boxes, straight lines, right angles throughout, black on white, each box listing
  what it takes in and what it makes. Download SVG saves that drawing. A capsule in the chart is
  drawn once as its own labelled flowchart above and appears as a single step below.


v5.0
Reviewed for professional laboratory use against the C6 construction-file reference
(github.com/UCB-BioE-Anderson-Lab/C6-Multiplatform) and the primary literature. Every
submission and decision is written up in REVIEW-v5.0.md.

- Ligation now models the 5' phosphate. T4 ligase seals a 5' phosphate to a 3' hydroxyl, so
  one side of every join has to carry one. Digest fragments arrive phosphorylated; a strand you
  wrote or amplified does not, and a new control in its inspector adds or removes them. This
  changes outcomes: re-ligating the fragments of a linear digest now gives the linear molecule
  rather than a phantom circle, which is what the reference gives and what the bench gives.
- PCR requires 18 bases of exact 3' match, the figure the reference uses, and refuses a primer
  that binds the template in more than one place instead of silently returning the first
  product.
- Golden Gate refuses a part carrying extra sites for the enzyme, naming their positions,
  rather than quietly cutting at the wrong one; and refuses a palindromic junction overhang,
  which would assemble in either orientation.
- Exported construction files run in the reference implementation. Sequences are declared with
  their type, so a plasmid stays circular and an assembly step is not handed a single strand;
  digest fragments are numbered from zero as the format specifies, not from one; and SOE and
  PCA, which the format has no operation for, are written as comments with their product
  declared, so the rest of the file still simulates.
- Designed primers grow until they reach 55 C rather than stopping at a flat 20 bases, and the
  card reports each primer's melting temperature.
- Ribosome binding sites are called ahead of GTG and TTG starts as well as ATG, which between
  them begin about one E. coli gene in nine.
- The library tabs fit their panel, the two databank buttons and the Sanger button say what
  they do in words, Golden Gate no longer claims every Type IIS enzyme leaves four bases, and a
  Sanger verdict's alignment gets room to be read.


v4.7
- Sanger reads. Open an .ab1 and the chromatogram opens with it: the four dye traces, the base
  called at each peak, and a readout under the pointer giving each nucleotide's share of the
  signal at that point and the confidence of the nearest call. Pick a range or view the whole
  read, and lay the confidence of each call or the dye signal at each peak behind the traces
  as bars.
- Auto-analyze grades a read against the strand it should be: Perfect, Point mutation,
  Deletion / insertion / other sequence, Perfect partial, or Bad read, with the position of
  every change and of anything the read could not reach.
- DNA Strider (.str) files open, and constructs and parts export as .str.
- The desktop download is built from the page the site is serving right now rather than from
  the tab you have open, so a tab left open for a week still hands you the current build.
- The match line between two aligned sequences sat two characters to the right of the bases it
  was about. It lines up now, in both pairwise alignments and BLAST hits.
- Pressing Enter in the registry or enzyme search puts every match on screen at once rather
  than a page at a time.


v4.6
- The iGEM Registry search reaches every match. The list used to stop at the 300 most-used
  parts; it now grows a page at a time as you scroll, so a search that finds thousands of parts
  can be scrolled through all the way down.
- New enzyme databank beside My enzymes: every restriction enzyme REBASE lists with a
  recognition sequence, searchable by name, organism or site, filterable to the ones whose cut
  is known, the ones a supplier sells, and the iGEM assembly-standard enzymes. Keep one and it
  lands in My enzymes ready to drag onto a strand.
- Recognition sites may now use the full IUPAC alphabet, so an enzyme like AccI (GT[AC][GT]AC)
  matches the bases it really means. Strands and parts are still A, C, G, T and N only.
- The sync light goes green again as soon as the file is written. It was repainted only when a
  change was queued, so it sat amber after every save.


v4.5
- The whole iGEM Registry rides along in the page, compressed: all 77,314 parts searchable from
  the Parts tab, with the sequence of every part up to 1,000 bp bundled. Keep one and it lands
  in My Parts as ordinary sequence.
- New Auto-annotate computation: strips a strand's annotations and reads a fresh set off the
  sequence, with a checklist for which structural features to look for and boxes for exact
  copies of registry or My Parts.
- The sync file is written on a heartbeat as well as on change, and closing the tab or the app
  with work it hasn't seen warns first.
- Downloading the app checks it's handing out this build, and offers to update a copy you
  already have by swapping only the page inside it rather than fetching the whole thing again.

- Experiments refuse to run on anything but A, C, G, T and N, and treat N as a wild card in
